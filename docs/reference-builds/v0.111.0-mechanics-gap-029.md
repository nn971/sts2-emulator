# v0.111.0 mechanics gap — pass 029

This pass moves the Act 1 slime work from isolated monster models to **source-backed encounter
formation construction**.

## Generic random slot groups

`PrototypeEncounterDefinition` can now combine:

- fixed enemy slots;
- one or more independent random-selection groups;
- explicit formation positions for each random group;
- optional distinct-within-group selection;
- optional slot names.

The resolver uses the existing combat RNG stream and returns one ordered list of concrete
`PrototypeEncounterEnemySpec` values.

It validates:

- non-empty pools;
- slot-name/position count agreement;
- enough candidates for a distinct request;
- unique final formation positions.

The older homogeneous `ChooseDistinct` encounter policy is also wired through the same concrete
formation path instead of remaining unused metadata.

This is a formation/content abstraction only. The prototype RNG codec and call schedule still do
not claim native encounter-construction RNG parity.

## `SLIMES_WEAK`

The native constructor chooses:

1. one of the two small slime types for the left/front small slot;
2. the other small slime type for the right/rear small slot;
3. one random medium slime for the middle slot.

The resulting source-backed composition is therefore:

- position 0: Leaf Slime (S) or Twig Slime (S);
- position 1: Leaf Slime (M) or Twig Slime (M);
- position 2: the other small slime.

The emulator represents the two small positions as one distinct selection group and the medium
position as an independent group.

## `SLIMES_NORMAL`

The native constructor fixes the two medium slimes and randomizes only the ordering of the two
different small slimes:

- position 0: Twig Slime (M);
- position 1: Leaf Slime (M);
- positions 2 and 3: Leaf Slime (S) and Twig Slime (S), in random order.

This maps directly to two fixed formation specs plus one distinct random group.

## Reference-only promotion

Both definitions are currently stored with `Weight = 0`.

That is deliberate. It means:

- their formation constructors are production code and directly testable;
- they do not silently enter the whole-run prototype encounter selector;
- the Act 1 completeness ledger may mark **model = yes** and **formation = yes**;
- **pool = pending** remains a separate claim.

Native weak/normal pool eligibility, anti-repeat rules, and exact encounter-selection RNG are the
next layer.

## Regressions

Focused tests sweep deterministic prototype seeds and verify:

- weak slimes always contain exactly two different small types and one medium type;
- both medium choices are reachable;
- both small-order permutations are reachable;
- normal slimes always begin with Twig M then Leaf M;
- the final two positions contain the two distinct small types;
- both small-order permutations are reachable;
- impossible distinct requests fail loudly.

## Next work

The next high-value Act 1 work can proceed on two parallel fronts:

1. **encounter pool semantics** — translate the Overgrowth weak/normal pool construction and its
   history constraints so the two slime encounters can enter a native Act 1 selector;
2. **next shared monster primitive** — summon/despawn/death behavior, which unlocks several remaining
   normal enemies and later elites.

Because all four slime models and both slime formations are now represented, pool translation is the
shortest route to the first genuinely complete native Act 1 encounters.
