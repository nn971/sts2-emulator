# Native Silent card rarity odds in Act 1 Overgrowth

The pinned v0.111.0 `CardRarityOdds` starts at a **-5 percentage point**
rare offset and persists across encounters and shops. Every generated
encounter-reward card rolls independently; rare resets the offset to -5 points,
while any other rarity raises it by +1 point on A0–A6, capped at +40 points.
Ascension 7 (Scarcity) changes the growth to +0.5 points and lowers the base
rare probability.

| Source | A0–A6 rare base | A7+ rare base | Uncommon base | Advances offset |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Regular encounter | 3% | 1.49% | 37% | Yes |
| Elite encounter | 10% | 5% | 40% | Yes |
| Boss encounter | 100% | 100% | 0% | Yes; resets |
| Merchant | 9% | 4.5% | 37% | No |

All percentages except the boss's rare chance are adjusted by the run's
current offset. Scarcity thresholds use the pinned `AscensionLevel.Scarcity = 7`.
When a tier is empty, `CardFactory.GetNextAllowedRarity` wraps
Common → Uncommon → Rare → Common. The offset update uses the
*initially rolled rarity* and therefore precedes tier fallback.

Native-structure Act 1 runs store
`RunWorldState.CardRarityOffsetBasisPoints`, generate every combat
card-reward group sequentially from that state, and let shops read it without
altering it. Configured Overgrowth and Underdocks runs, the explicitly
opted-in experimental Hive/Glory route, and active Golden Path profiles call
these provider-backed reward helpers. The Hive/Glory route remains
experimental; shared helper use does not establish source pool or full
later-act reward parity. Configured combat reward rarity, model, and upgrade
draws use the persistent player Rewards stream. Merchant
rarity also uses Rewards, while merchant card selection and upgrade checks use
Shops. The source performs an upgrade check for every generated combat reward
card; the Act 1 baseline chance is zero.

## Per-option upgrades and visibility

`CardFactory.RollForUpgrade` reads the **zero-indexed** act number.
Ordinary Common and Uncommon combat rewards gain a 12.5% upgrade
chance per act after Act 1 at A0–A6, or 25% per act after Act 1 at
A7+ (Scarcity). Rare and non-upgradable cards have zero chance.
Every reward upgrade attempt consumes one Rewards RNG draw, even if
the card cannot upgrade. Native uses a float comparison with an
exactly-zero endpoint; the prototype discrete sampler models a
zero-percent chance as exactly zero.

The Silver Crucible override upgrades every option in its reward group,
including Rare cards. Its per-group flag composes by OR with each
option's own generated upgrade flag. Main and additional reward groups
now preserve per-option flags; chosen cards use their specific flag,
while skipped cards leave the player's deck unchanged. Forks deep-copy
the flags; reward invariants validate their lengths.

The `prototype-ai-v0` observation includes
`Reward.CardOptionUpgradeFlags`, aligned with the *currently shown*
`Reward.CardOptions` for the player's reward choice. Future reward
groups and RNG rolls remain private. The native Overgrowth profile currently
runs in Act 1, so ordinary upgrade flags are false unless another modifier
(such as Silver Crucible) upgrades the group. The helper accepts higher-act
parameters and is reached by the experimental Hive/Glory and Golden Path
routes, but its source reward pools and full Native call-prefix parity remain
open.

Configured runs now use native float/index providers for these player-stream
operations, but the restricted Silent pool still replaces native unlock-state
filtering and future hooks. Seeded result/cursor parity remains unverified.
Unconfigured prototype runs retain the discrete integer samplers; other
prototype region/act generation retains its existing weights.

Read-only `chance_law_query` also exposes the raw `card_rarity.roll.unit_float`
law for a caller-declared source, current offset, Ascension, owner and stream
ordinal. It counts exact source-rounded Native `NextFloat()` grid inputs at the
source thresholds before `GetNextAllowedRarity` applies any available-pool
fallback. The query does not validate the encounter reward prefix or card
pool, and does not establish stream assignment or cursor parity.

The query also exposes `card_rarity.upgrade_roll.unit_float` for an implemented
Native Silent reward card, act, Ascension and stream ordinal. Its outcomes are
the raw comparison flag under the source's inclusive
`NextFloat() <= threshold` check, before any card mutation. At a zero threshold,
the single 53-bit grid input that rounds to `0.0f` still passes; the query
counts that exact edge and does not claim that every passing flag can upgrade
its card.

Encounter card-model selection also has a cursor-blind `NextInt` law over a
caller-declared remaining support size, owner, and Rewards-stream ordinal.
The exact bounded-index weights do not validate the ordered card pool,
reward prefix, stream assignment, or cursor parity.
